Problem statement

The prevalence of at-risk gambling among Australian adults increased from 7.3% in 2018 to 9.8% in 2022 across low-, moderate- and high-risk categories (HILDA). Greater exposure to gambling risk is associated with gambling-related harm and wider social costs. Despite these harms, gambling generates substantial revenue for operators and state and territory governments, creating potentially competing incentives between maintaining gambling activity and implementing effective harm-reduction measures.

The challenge is to prevent individuals from progressing into higher-risk categories while reducing harm across all levels of risk — without creating unintended consequences or overlooking the competing interests that sustain the system. Low-risk gamblers matter too: there are many more of them, and Australian research finds they already have worse health, social and economic outcomes than non-gamblers.

A wicked problem: government revenue vs. public-health responsibility vs. operators' commercial interests. The model does not assume governments avoid regulation because of revenue — it shows how the tension plays out over time.
